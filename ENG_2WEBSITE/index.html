Lecture 1

Technical Writing: Definition and Purpose

What is technical writing?

Technical writing communicates specialized or practical information in a structured, objective, and understandable way. It commonly uses precise vocabulary, graphic aids, and conventional document formats.

Why do people write technically?

  • To inform: explain facts, instructions, or findings.
  • To persuade or influence: present reasons for a proposed action or decision.
  • To analyze events: examine information and explain what it shows.
Example

Instead of “The device is acting weird,” write: “The device shuts down approximately two minutes after it is turned on.” The second sentence states an observable, specific issue.

TECHNICAL NOTE

Device Check

Purpose: report an observed issue

Observation

The device shuts down approximately two minutes after it is turned on.

Next step

Record the conditions and check the power connection.

ABC's of Technical Writing

The "ALPHABET" of technical writing principles. These selected principles are especially useful when checking a document before sharing it.

A

Accuracy

Record information and data correctly; check names, figures, and claims.

B

Brevity

Be direct and concise without removing information readers need.

C

Confidence

Communicate decisively when the evidence supports the statement.

D

Dignity

Show courtesy and respect for readers.

E

Emphasis

Make the information most important to the reader easy to find.

F

Facility

Use helpful devices and organization to make the report easy to understand.

G

Grammatical correctness

Use correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation.

H

Honesty

Acknowledge sources and do not misrepresent information.

I

Illustration

Use relevant tables, charts, graphs, or diagrams to clarify information.

J

Judgement

Qualify data and information gathered.

K

Knowledge

Collection of data and information.

L

Logic

Process of qualification.

M

Mechanical Neatness

General Appearance of the report.

N

Normal Procedure

Conformity to general standard.

O

Objectivity

Present facts fairly rather than relying on personal preference.

P

Planning

Give the document purpose and direction before drafting.

Q

Qualification

Evaluate ideas to be included.

R

Revision

Review statements, spelling, punctuation, and organization.

S

Straight Sentence

Vary sentence structure and use of transitional device.

T

Thoroughness

Substance of report.

U

Unity

Coherence of ideas.

V

Viewpoint

Perspective in Writing.

W

Word Choice

Avoid words difficult to understand.

Z

Zest

Write things that are invigorating.

EXAMPLE: Before and after

Vague “A lot of people liked the new process.”

Specific “In the sample survey, 18 of 25 respondents preferred the new process.”

Outlining the Subject

Limit the topic first

Limiting a subject means narrowing a broad topic to a manageable focus for a particular purpose and audience. An outline then arranges the main ideas and supporting details in a logical hierarchy.

How to make an outline

  1. Identify the topic and purpose.
  2. Determine the main ideas.
  3. Arrange the ideas in a logical order.
  4. Add supporting details.
  5. Review and revise the structure.

Two common forms: a topic outline uses short phrases; a sentence outline expresses ideas in complete sentences.

Example:
from broad to focused

Technology in education
Online learning
How weekly online quizzes support review in one class

Alphanumeric outline example

I. Main topic

A. Main idea

1. Supporting detail

a. Example or explanation

B. Second main idea

The hierarchy shows how details support broader ideas.

Types of Technical Paper

Technical writing appears in different document forms. The form depends on the information being communicated and the readers who need it.

01

Technical report

Organizes data, methods, analysis, findings, and conclusions about a body of work.

02

Instructional manual

Gives directions for carrying out a procedure or using equipment.

03

Proposal

Recommends a plan or action and explains why it should be approved.

04

Progress report

Records what has been accomplished on a project and its current status.

05

Abstract

Provides a short, standalone summary of a larger document.

06

Memorandum (memo)

Communicates information within an organization, commonly with To, From, Date, and Subject fields.

07

Feasibility report

Examines whether a proposed project or plan is practical and worthwhile.

08

Laboratory report

Records the procedure and results of a laboratory test or experiment.

09

Survey report

Presents findings from a systematic study, such as a survey of opinions.

10

Technical paper / journal article

Discusses a technical subject, theory, research, or development for a professional readership.

11

Specification

States detailed requirements for performance, materials, construction, or operation.

12

Business letter

Communicates formally with an external recipient; examples include inquiry, order, reply, complaint, and application letters.

How to choose

Use an instructional manual when readers need steps to complete a task. Use a progress report when readers need an account of what has been completed so far. Choose the document form that matches the communication purpose.

Lecture 5

The Basic English Structure

A complete English sentence normally expresses a complete thought. A common basic pattern is Subject + Verb; other patterns add an object, complement, or adverbial information.

SUBJECT The technician

Who or what the sentence is about

+
VERB checked

The action or state

+
OBJECT the cable

Receives the action

The technician checked the cable.

SV

Subject + Verb
The alarm sounded.

SVO

Subject + Verb + Object
The technician checked the cable.

SVC

Subject + Verb + Complement
The reading is accurate.

SVA

Subject + Verb + Adjunct
The team worked carefully.

Technical-writing tip

Prefer a complete, direct sentence. Fragment: “After checking the cable.” Complete sentence: “The technician restarted the device after checking the cable.”

Parts of a Letter

A standard formal business letter uses recognizable parts so the reader can identify the sender, purpose, message, and next action.

1. Sender's address

Technical Writing Student
College / Institution
City, Philippines

2. Date

10 October 2026

3. Recipient's address

Course Instructor
Department / Institution
City, Philippines

4. Salutation

Dear Instructor:

5. Body

I am submitting the attached technical report for your review. It summarizes the procedure followed and the observations recorded during the activity.

6. Complimentary closing

Sincerely,

7. Signature and typed name

Katrina Joyce D. Ignacio

Student

Parts explained

  1. Sender's address: identifies where the letter comes from.
  2. Date: indicates when it was written.
  3. Recipient's address: identifies the intended recipient.
  4. Salutation: formal greeting.
  5. Body: states the purpose and provides the message.
  6. Closing: ends the letter courteously.
  7. Signature / typed name: identifies the sender.

Graphs / Charts

Graphs and charts present numerical information visually. They can make comparisons, trends, and proportions easier to recognize when the data and labels are clear.

Bar graph

Compares values across separate categories.

Group A4
Group B7
Group C5
Illustrative example only.

Line graph

Shows a trend or change across a continuous sequence, often time.

Illustrative example only.

Pie chart

Shows how categories contribute to a whole (100%).

  • Category A — 50%
  • Category B — 30%
  • Category C — 20%
Illustrative proportions only; not real research data.
Rules for useful visuals
  • Give every graph a clear title and label the axes or categories.
  • Use scales that represent the values fairly.
  • Identify the source when data come from another source.
  • Place the visual near the text that explains it.
  • Use a graph to clarify information, not merely to decorate the page.
